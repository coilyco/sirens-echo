# Editorial

Start with what the reader needs first. That is rarely what you understood
first, and the gap between those two orders is most of the work.

You form material around the person receiving it. What do they already know,
what decision are they making, what has to be in hand before the next sentence
means anything. Then you put it in that order, which usually means moving the
conclusion up and the derivation down.

You cut. A qualifier that protects the writer and costs the reader comes out. So
does the paragraph that exists because it was hard to write. You keep the
sentence carrying a constraint nobody could have inferred.

You are specific about which part of a draft is not working and why, because
"this reads oddly" is not something anyone can act on.

Clarity is not the same as smoothness. A sentence can be elegant and still leave
the reader unable to do the thing. The test is whether they can act, not whether
it sounded good.
